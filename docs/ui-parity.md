# UI parity

> **Last reviewed:** 2026-10-31

How DiaCraft's interaction compares with the target. The target is the reference app's desktop edition. "The command exists" is not enough: it has to accept the same input and behave the same way.

**Headline:** no parity yet (estimated). The reference app was not inspected, nothing was launched or measured, and our output has not been opened in it by the project. What follows says where things stand; the layout and the names follow the reference app's, and the look (colours, icons, sizes) is DiaCraft's own.

## What exists

The window opens on an empty new drawing, or on the file named on the command line (`diacraft FILE`), and is laid out as the spec has it: a title bar (Quick Access Toolbar with Save, Undo and Redo, the drawing's name and the application's, a search box, the account), a tab row (File, Home, Insert, Draw, Design, Data, Process, Review, View, Help; Developer when turned on; Comments and Share at the right end; the Org Chart tab shows after View and Developer, before Help, while the drawing holds an org chart; the Cross-Functional Flowchart tab after Help while the page has swimlanes; and Container Format at the right end while a container is selected), the ribbon body with the groups of each tab in the map's order, the Shapes window with a compact stencil chooser, current-stencil filter and square icon cards, the drawing area with rulers, a grid and the page, page tabs, a status bar, and the File view. The tabs and groups of the map for a plain drawing are drawn except four groups, Flow on Process, Insights on Review, View Direction on View and the legacy Themes group on Design, and seven tabs, Add-ins, Stencil and Icon Editor and four contextual tabs (Picture Format, Data Visualizer Design, Ink Tools Pens and ShapeSheet Design). A group that does not fit the window gives up size (small icon buttons, then one button that opens the group) before it clips. Colours come through theme tokens, light by default, with a dark set kept.

**What works** (a control is enabled only when a registered command stands behind it; a test holds the ribbon to that):

- **Drawing** (`docs/editing-commands.md` has the commands it sends). A tile of the Shapes window dragged onto the page drops its shape there (`shape.drop`), snapped to the grid; a click on a tile drops one at the middle of what is in view. Every actual master of the stencil shown is enabled when a drawing is open; unavailable placeholder tiles are omitted. With the **Pointer Tool** a click selects (Shift or Ctrl adds or drops one), a drag on bare page is a selection net that takes what lies wholly inside (a translucent light-grey veil with a one-pixel grey outline, drawn over the shapes from the first movement and gone at the release; nothing is marked on a shape until then, and the pointer is a crosshair from the press; the Advanced option "Select shapes partially within area" is not built), Esc and a click on bare page clear, Ctrl+A selects all. A drag moves the selection (`shape.move`; Shift holds it to one axis, Alt turns off the snap); the arrow keys nudge it one grid step, Shift one point. **A selection is drawn as the reference application draws it** (every colour is a theme token and every size is in screen points, `theme::select`): one shape or one group selected has a thin grey box on its own box, eight round white handles with a grey ring (four on a shape drawn small) and a rotation handle (a circular arrow on a stem, its centre 36 points above the top edge); several shapes have a dark blue frame on each shape's box, 2 points wide and 4 on the primary (the one selected first), a connector's own path drawn again in that blue, and one thin grey box round them all, 16 points off the outermost shapes, with the same eight handles and the rotation handle. A drag of a single 2-D shape's or a group's handle resizes it (`shape.resize`; Shift on a corner keeps the proportions; the members of a group follow it) and the rotation handle turns it; **the handles round several shapes work too**: a corner stretches them all about the opposite corner in proportion to their joint box, a side stretches them in one axis (in proportion with Shift), the rotation handle turns them as one about the middle of the joint box in steps that get finer the farther the pointer is, the outlines are previewed while the button is down, and the release sends `shape.resize` with `ids` or `shape.rotate` with `by` and `about`, one undo step; the pointer over a handle is the two-headed arrow of its direction (diagonal at a corner) and the grabbing hand over the rotation handle, and a shape locked against sizing or turning is left alone. A selected connector has its two ends as handles, a green disc where glued and a square where free, and a drag of one glues it elsewhere or frees it (`connector.reconnect`). Delete removes the selection and the connectors glued to it (`shape.delete`). While the pointer is down the window paints a light preview of the moving outlines over the last picture and sends one command on release; the new picture follows. The **Connector** tool (Home, Tools; Ctrl+3) joins two shapes by a drag from one to the other (`connector.add`), shows the connection points as small marks and lights the one under the pointer (the shape, when it is over the body); it stays on until the Pointer Tool (Ctrl+1) or Esc. Double-click, F2, or typing on a selected shape opens a text box over it; Esc, Ctrl+Enter or a click away keeps the text (`shape.set_text`). **The box holds the shape's characters, and its fields are shown under them as pieces that cannot be typed into; only the characters that changed are sent, so the formatting runs and the fields of a label survive a changed letter**. A box that is open is sent before a page change, Undo, Redo, Save, New, Open, Export or a close, so a label being typed is never lost. The status bar gives the selected shape's width, height and angle. Every edit is one undo step, so Undo and Ctrl+Z cover it all.
- **A connector's path** (`docs/editing-commands.md`, "The window's connector path handles"). With the Pointer Tool and one right-angle connector selected, a blue disc at the middle of each leg that is long enough on the screen is a handle: a drag moves the leg across (the cursor is the arrow across it), the engine's snap pulls it, the route the engine will write is drawn dashed while the button is down, Alt turns the snap off, Escape drops the drag, and the release is one `connector.edit_path` (one undo step; the connector keeps its glue and is marked as edited by hand, `ConFixedCode` 3). **The connector's right-click menu** has Cut, Copy, Paste, Delete, Bring to Front, Send to Back, **Reset Connector** and a **Reroute** menu of four check boxes (`connector.reroute`: Reroute Freely, Reroute As Needed, Never Reroute, Reroute on Crossover), each one undo step. Not built: Shift with an arrow key moving a handle a pixel at a time, a handle on a straight or a curved connector, Edit Text in the connector's menu.
- **Formatting and arranging** (`docs/editing-commands.md`, "Formatting and arranging from the window"). With a shape or several selected, the Home tab's **Font** group (a list of the machine's fonts found off the window's thread, a Size list that takes any size, Increase and Decrease, Bold, Italic, Underline, Strikethrough, Font Color), **Paragraph** group (the three places of the text in its block, left, centre, right, justify, **Bullets**, a split button whose icon turns the bullet on or off and whose arrow chooses one of the seven marks, **Decrease Indent** and **Increase Indent**, a quarter inch a step, and **Left-to-Right**, **Right-to-Left** and **Vertical Text**, `docs/editing-commands.md`, "Bullets, indents, Change Case and text direction from the window") and the Font group's **Change Case** menu (Normal Case, All Caps, Initial Caps, Small Caps), **Shape Styles** group (Fill and Line with a palette, No Fill, No Line, weights, dashes and all 46 line ends, each drawn as the page draws it; the list of 23 dashes is as tall as the window lets it be, all of it on the screen in a window 800 points tall or more, and in a shorter window it scrolls with a bar that is always drawn; Fill Options..., Line Options... and More Arrows... open the Format Shape pane on its Fill & Line tab) and **Arrange** group (Align, Position with Rotate Right, Rotate Left, Flip Horizontal and Flip Vertical, Bring to Front and Send to Back with Bring Forward and Send Backward) show the state of the selection and send one command; a mixed selection shows a half pressed toggle and a blank box. The keys Ctrl+B, I, U, Ctrl+Shift+> and <, Ctrl+Shift+L, C, R, J, T, M, V, Ctrl+Shift+A, K, Ctrl+L, R, H, J and Ctrl+Shift+F, B do the same. With a text box open the controls format the characters selected in it and the box stays open. A selected 2-D shape or group has a **rotation handle**; a turned or flipped shape has its **handles** on its own box; a second click on a selected group selects the member under the pointer. With a text box open the Font group reads the characters selected (the run before the caret for a caret): a half pressed toggle and a blank box where the runs differ (gap U19 settled). **Clipboard, duplicate and group** ("Clipboard, duplicate and group from the window"): Paste (a split button), Cut and Copy of the Clipboard group, Duplicate (Paste Duplicate), and Group and Ungroup of the Arrange group, are enabled when they apply, with the keys Ctrl+C, X, V, D, Ctrl+Shift+G and U; a drag started with Ctrl held copies the shapes; a right click on the page opens Cut, Copy, Paste (at the click), Duplicate, Group and Ungroup; the system clipboard gets the labels' text. Add to Group, Remove from Group and Convert to Group are drawn and quiet (gap U20). **Format Painter** (`painter.rs`, the window's `format.painter`): a press on the button, or Ctrl+Shift+P, copies the look of the shape selected (`shape.formatting`: fill, line, weight, dash pattern, both ends and rounding, and the text's face, size, styles, colour and alignment) and shows a copy cursor; the next press on a shape paints it (`shape.format` and `text.format` as one `command.batch`, one undo step) and puts the painter down, and a press on a member of a group paints the whole group; a double click on the button keeps the painter on until Escape, the Pointer Tool or another tool (the double click rests on one tutorial); with two shapes or more selected the first is copied and the others are painted at once; a shape that is in a group is not copied from. **Stencil files**: More Shapes, Open Stencil shows the app's file dialog and opens the file; the stencil has a chooser entry with a close control, is the one shown, and has a tile for each master that is not hidden, drawn from its icon or its own outline; a tile drops its shape like a built-in one, and a master the engine refuses is told in the status bar. The Font box and the Text dialog's face box name the face the shape is drawn in, blank only where the shapes differ.
- **Saving** (the commands). Save (the toolbar, the File view, Ctrl+S) runs `file.save`, or, for a drawing with no file yet, shows the system's Save dialog and runs `file.save_as`; Save As (the File view, Ctrl+Shift+S) always asks. **The kinds the Save dialog offers** (`save_kinds.rs`): the drawing's own type first, which the dialog selects (`.diacraft` for a new drawing, and what it was read as or last saved as for another), then the types the engine says it writes for this drawing, which the window asks of it (`Session::save_as_allows`, the save-path table asked without saving: the window keeps no table of its own), in the design's order: DiaCraft's drawing and template, the OpenDocument drawing and its template (`.odg`, `.otg`: added `.otg`, which the Open dialog lists too), then the VSDX types, each under its file-type label, which names the product of the VSDX types (owner 2026-11-23); DiaCraft's own types and the OpenDocument ones keep their labels. A stencil is offered `.diacraft-stencil` and the VSDX stencil types, and no OpenDocument type (OpenDocument has no stencil form). Every other drawing is offered all of them, since the engine writes each across the formats; a type the engine refuses for what a drawing holds is still offered, and its refusal is told in words. Save As switches the drawing to the file written, so the title is that file's name after it (Call 15). The title carries a star while there are changes that are not saved (the engine's own answer: an edit and its undo leave it clean). New, Open (once a file is chosen) and closing the window ask first, with Save, Don't Save and Cancel, when there are unsaved changes. A refusal (a link where the file should go, a name with no drawing extension, a full disk) is told in plain words and the drawing stays unsaved. **The kind chosen sets the extension**: the Save dialog starts with a name that has the extension of the first kind, which is the kind it selects, and choosing another kind in its list changes the extension of the name to that kind's, on each system (the app pins `rfd` to a fork that does it: the macOS panel, the Linux portal and the Windows dialog each have a part), unless the person typed an extension of their own, which is kept; Export and Print's Save as PDF go through the same dialog. On macOS the panel changes the extension of the name it returns to the preferred extension of the type it is given, and which type an extension resolves to depends on the applications installed (an application can declare a type for `.vsdx` that prefers `.vsd`), so a kind whose type prefers another extension is given to the panel as no type, and the fork adds the kind's extension to a returned name that has none. The panel can return another extension even with the extension shown, so the name field's extension is used then, and when a file already has that name the person is asked whether to replace it (Replace or Cancel, Cancel the default; Cancel ends the save as if the dialog had been cancelled), since the panel asked only about the name it returned. **Two questions more**: a name the dialog still returned with no file type (a dialog that sets none, such as the zenity fallback on Linux) gets the drawing's own type (`.diacraft` for a new drawing, `.png` for an export), and if that file is there the window asks Replace or Cancel; a file that changed on the disk since the drawing was opened is not replaced without asking: Overwrite, Save As or Cancel; **a Save As the engine refuses because the drawing holds a macro project and the type chosen (`.vsdx`, `.vssx`, `.vstx`) cannot hold it** is asked about in the engine's own words, "Leave the macros out?": Save without macros runs `file.save_as` again with `drop_macros`, **Save as .vsdm** (`.vssm` for a stencil, `.vstm` for a template: the macro-enabled type of the one chosen) runs it onto that name and keeps the project, asking Replace or Cancel first when a file of that name is there, and Cancel (or Escape) leaves the drawing and its file as they were and ends what waited on the save (a New, an Open or the close does not happen); a program that replaces the drawing under any save question (`file.new`, `file.open`) closes it the same way (a program that asks the same save gets the error and no card). **The save note** (`save_note.rs`): a save across the VSDX and OpenDocument formats that carries data the type has no place for, a macro project, embedded objects or digital signatures is told in a card over the top of the drawing area, headed "A note about" and the file's name, in the engine's own words for the result's `carried` field (`across::words`, the design's 5.6 exactly, one paragraph for each part that applies in the design's order; the window never reads a warning). It is shown once for the save and read in full (it is as tall as its words, and scrolls when the window gives it less room), dismissed with its button, and it blocks nothing: the file is written before it shows, and the keys and the pointer under it work. The next save replaces it (one that carries nothing takes it away), and a New or an Open does too; under the banner of the ODF decision-8 note (once in a session) when both are up. The reference application's own way, the Compatibility Checker, asks Continue or Cancel before a save into an earlier version's type; it is not followed, because nothing is lost (the file keeps what it carries, and the note warns that other programs may leave it out), because the engine knows what a save carries only from the save, and because a program that runs the same command gets the answer in its result and no question (unverified against the reference application, which was not inspected). The questions, and the progress card of a file being read, hold the window: nothing under them takes the pointer, and the shortcuts and drawing keys are held (under the File view only New, Open, Save and Save As run).

- **Stencils**. The Shapes window asks the engine which stencils there are (`stencil.list`) and shows the active library in a compact chooser above its master cards. The chooser popup is **More Shapes**: it files every built-in stencil under its category (General, Flowchart, Software and Database), puts a check mark on each that is open, and a click on one that is not open opens it (`stencil.open {id}`) and shows it, so a drawing made from a template can open the stencils the template left out; it switches stencils (`window.stencil {stencil}` does the same); cards form a fixed-square grid, two columns at the default panel width. The figures of Basic Shapes are drawn in code; the masters of any other stencil (Basic Flowchart) are drawn from their own outlines, read once from the engine, so a stencil the engine gains appears with no change in the window.
- **Export**. Export in the File view, and Picture in the View tab's Export group, show the system's Save dialog, filtered to PNG, SVG and PDF, and write the page on view with `file.export {path, page}`; the kind chosen sets the extension of the name as in Save As, and a name that still has no extension gets `.png`. The picture is made on a thread of its own from a copy of the drawing, so the window never waits and the drawing is never touched (an export is no undo step and does not mark the drawing saved). A refusal (a name that is none of the three, a folder that is not there, a full disk) is told in plain words over the drawing. Open and Export are disabled where the app has no file dialog (the web).
- **Print**. Print in the File view (after Save As) and Ctrl+P show the Print page: the settings in a column at the left, the preview filling the right with a sheet control and a zoom control under it (below 700 points of width the preview goes under the settings). The settings are the printer (the system's, listed on a thread of its own, or Save as PDF), copies, All pages or Current page, Collated, two-sided printing and Color or Black and white (for a printer and for Save as PDF alike: black and white draws the preview again in greys at once, and the PDF has every colour as its grey), and under them what the page on view says of its paper, its scale and its sheets (Page Setup's cells, read, not set here). The preview is one sheet of the print as the printer is given it (`print_preview.rs`), drawn off the window's thread, with a faint line where its margins are; a page that takes several sheets has a map of them at the corner of the preview, and the sheet control, the map and Page Up and Page Down move between sheets. Print makes the PDF of each run of sheets that share a paper (`file.print`'s own, a page for each sheet at the paper's size) and hands it to the desktop's `PrintService` (`craft-print`: CUPS, or the Windows spooler) on a thread, with Cancel until it reaches the spooler; Save as PDF shows a Save dialog and writes the same file with `file.print`. The commands `print.set` and `print.start` do what the controls do. A window with no service (the web) offers the PDF alone. Nothing here has been sent to a real printer by the project's tests (gaps U23).
- **Page setup** (the commands are `docs/editing-commands.md`, "Page setup"). The Design tab's Page Setup group: **Orientation** (a menu of Portrait and Landscape, the way the page is held marked; `page.orientation`, for the page on view: a background page is set up by showing it), **Size** (the engine's named sizes in their groups with their dimensions, the page's size marked, a scroll when the window is short; `page.size`; Fit to Drawing is drawn quiet, More Page Sizes opens the dialog) and **Auto Size** (a toggle, pressed while the page grows by itself; `page.auto_size`). A size or an orientation chosen there puts the whole page in view. The **Page Setup dialog** (the group's launcher, More Page Sizes, the page tab's menu, the File view's Print page, and `window.pageSetup`) has the six tabs of the spec in order. Print Setup (the printer paper from `page.presets` and the way it is held: `page.paper`; print zoom, sheets, margins, centring and the grid are drawn quiet, with the values the page's cells hold, since the engine has no command that writes them), Page Size (a named size or a custom size in inches, and the orientation: `page.size`, `page.orientation`), Drawing Scale (a listed scale or a custom one with units: `page.scale`) and Page Properties (the name: `page.rename`; **Type**, Foreground or Background: `page.type`; **Background**, None or one of the drawing's background pages: `page.background`) work; Layout and Routing and Alt Text are drawn quiet and cannot be opened. **The dialog works on a draft:** OK sends what changed, as one command or as one `command.batch` that is one undo step, and Cancel and Esc send nothing; a refusal (a size that is not a number, a page taken away under the dialog) is told in the dialog, which stays. With Auto Size on, a drop or a move past the edge can grow the page and, past the left or the bottom, carry every shape: the window reads the drawing again, and a view of its own (not Fit) follows the carried drawing, so that nothing jumps on the screen. **The page tabs:** the add control sends `page.add` with `like` the page on view; a right click opens the menu of the spec (Insert, Delete, Rename, Duplicate, Page Setup, a divider, Reorder Pages), every row wired: Delete is enabled with two pages and quiet on the only page that is not a background, Reorder Pages needs two pages that are not backgrounds, Duplicate copies the page (`page.duplicate`) and shows the copy, and a quiet row says why in its tooltip; Rename types the name in place (Enter or a click away keeps it, Esc leaves it); a tab of a page that is not a background dragged to another place among them sends `page.move`; **Reorder Pages** (Ctrl+Alt+P) has Move Up, Move Down and a box that renames default names for the places they take. The window commands are `window.pageSetup` and `window.reorderPages`.
- **Transparency, patterns, gradients, shadows and Quick Styles** (`docs/editing-commands.md`, "Transparency, patterns, gradients, shadows and Quick Styles"). The **Format Shape** pane's Fill has No fill, Solid fill, Gradient fill and Pattern fill (a number box and a slider for the transparency; for a gradient the layout, the angle or the place it starts from, a bar with a handle for each stop, Add stop, Remove stop and the colour, position and transparency of the stop selected; for a pattern a grid of 23 patterns and the colours of the marks and the ground), its Line has No line, Solid line and Gradient line with the transparency, compound type and cap type, and its Effects tab has the **Shadow** group (Presets, Color, Transparency, Size, Blur, Angle, Distance). **A slider shows its value on the page as it is dragged and is one undo step.** Home, Shape Styles: **Quick Styles** opens the Variant Styles and the Theme Styles (46 tiles drawn from the shape in each style; the tile the shape has is marked), **Fill** has Gradient, Pattern and Transparency submenus, **Line** a Transparency submenu, and **Effects, Shadow** the ten presets and Shadow Options.... The Format Painter carries all of it. Reflection, Glow, Soft Edges, Bevel and 3-D Rotation stay quiet (gap U41).
- **The colour palette and the theme preview** (`docs/editing-commands.md`, "The colour palette and the theme preview"). The Fill, Line and Font Color menus open with Use Default, Theme Colors, Variant Colors, Standard Colors, Recent Colors, No Fill or No Line and More Colors (the Colors dialog), and a tile of the Themes gallery, of the Variants strip or a row of its Colors menu shows its theme on the page while the pointer is on it (no undo step, no unsaved mark). The Format Shape pane's colour buttons draw the same palette.
- **Themes, Variants and Connectors** (`docs/editing-commands.md`, "Page setup and text from the window", the DESIGN rules). The Design tab's **Themes** gallery shows the first three themes of `theme.list` as tiles drawn in code from each theme's own colours (a small "Aa" over two overlapping shapes and a row of the six accents); a press applies the theme to the page on view (`theme.apply`), a right click offers the current page and all pages, and the tile of the page's theme is pressed. The arrow opens the whole list, with **No Theme** (`theme.apply` with `none`, marked while the page has none); Apply Theme to New Shapes is drawn quiet (the engine has no setting for it). **Variants** shows the four variants of the page's theme and picks one for the colours and the styles together (`theme.variant`); its **Colors** menu picks the colours' variant alone, which is our reading of a menu whose list of colour sets the engine cannot give a page; Effects, Connectors, Embellishments and Create New Theme Colors are drawn quiet. Variants is quiet until the page has a theme. **A theme is not previewed while the pointer is over its tile**: the engine cannot show a theme without editing the drawing, so a tile names the theme in a tooltip and nothing more. **Layout > Connectors** has Right-Angle, Straight Lines and Curved Lines (`connector.style`, on the connectors selected, or the page's default for new connectors when none is, the page's default marked) and the **Show Line Jumps** check box (`connector.line_jumps`, the page's, on in a new drawing). Re-Layout Page and its More Layout Options work (`window.relayout`, `window.relayoutOptions`); the Layout and Routing launcher, Backgrounds and Borders & Titles stay quiet.
- **Panes and the Text dialog** (the commands are `docs/control-protocol.md`, `window.pane` and the others beside it). View, Show, Task Panes (and F3 for Format Shape, the launcher of Shape Styles, and the page's menu) open a **dock at the right of the drawing area**, 280 points wide at first and as wide as the person drags it (200 points up to 45% of the window). **Several panes stack in it, each with a title and a close button**; a pane's `...` menu makes it **share a space with another pane, with tabs along the bottom**, take a space of its own, or move its space up or down. **Size & Position** (its title holds the shape's name) shows X, Y, Width, Height, Angle and a Pin Pos grid of nine places in the page's unit, read with `shape.placement`; a typed value, on Enter or on leaving the field, is one command (`shape.place` for X and Y, `shape.size`, `shape.rotate`, `shape.pin`), and a field takes units and arithmetic (`21 in`, `1 ft. 5 in.`) and refuses a comma in words under the field; several shapes show a field blank where they differ and a size typed is the size of all; a line shows Begin X and Y, End X and Y, Length and Angle (`shape.set_end`, `shape.set_length`), the fields of a glued end and the length of a line glued at both ends quiet. **Format Shape** has Fill & Line, which shows the selection and sends one `format.shape` for each change (so the page is the preview), and Effects, which is quiet. **Pan & Zoom** draws the page with the outlines of its shapes (every shape has at least its box; the picture says under itself when some are drawn simplified) and a rectangle for the part in view: drag the rectangle to pan, a corner of it to zoom, press the page to centre the view there, or use the slider. **Drawing Explorer** is a tree of the pages (a row goes to the page), styles, masters and patterns, in the order of R20. **The Text dialog** (the launchers of the Font and Paragraph groups, F11) has Font, Paragraph and Text Block tabs and sends one `text.format` on OK, for the shapes selected or the characters selected in the open text box; Cancel sends nothing. **A value typed in a field goes to the shapes the field showed when it took the focus**, whatever is selected when it lets go of the focus (a press on another shape, on the empty page or in another field, Tab, Enter); **a click anywhere but in the box ends the edit at the top of the frame, before the title bar, the ribbon or the File view act on it**, so a Save, an Undo or an Open that the click makes finds the value in the drawing, and a drawing opened over the open one takes none of it; a box nothing was typed in sends nothing and follows the drawing (with all its text selected again), and Esc leaves the old value. Every enabled control of the panes sends one registered command, except the Fill & Line and Effects tabs of Format Shape, which are view state like the tabs of a shared space; what no command sets is drawn quiet (gaps U25). The Font box and the Text dialog's face box name the face the shape is drawn in, blank only where the shapes differ.
- **The Text tool and the Text Block tool**. **Text** (Home, Tools; Insert, Text Box, Horizontal Text Box; Ctrl+2): a click on bare page opens a box where it was clicked, a drag opens one of the size dragged (both on the grid, Alt turns it off), and a click on a shape types into that shape (`shape.set_text`); **nothing is sent until the edit ends with characters in it**, when one `text.box` goes with the text (one undo step, no empty box is ever left), and the new box is selected. The tool is used up by the box it opens and the Pointer Tool is back. **Text Block** (Home, Tools; Ctrl+Shift+4): a click selects a shape and the block of its text is drawn over it from `text.block` (its rectangle, the pin it turns about, eight handles and a rotation handle; the shape's own handles are not shown); a drag of the body is `text.block_move`, a drag of a handle `text.block_resize` and a drag of the rotation handle `text.block_rotate` (in the steps of the shape's own rotation handle), each sent on release as one undo step and drawn from a preview while the button is down; the tool stays on until Esc or the Pointer Tool. **Rotate Text** (Home, Paragraph; `format.rotateText`) turns the block of every shape selected by a quarter, counterclockwise (`text.block_rotate` with `by` 90; several shapes are one undo step). The window commands are `tool.text`, `tool.text_block` and `format.rotateText`, and the window state says which tool is on and where the block shown is (`textBlock`).

## Stencil gallery

**The stencil gallery was accepted by the owner on 2026-11-13.** A compact active-stencil chooser replaces the permanent list of stencil rows. It shows the library name and actual master count; its popup retains stencil selection, imported-stencil closing and Open Stencil. Unavailable Quick Shapes and placeholder masters are omitted. The catalogue is seven stencils and fifty-eight actual masters (the Cross-Functional Flowchart Shapes and the Organization Chart Shapes came after it).

The scrollable gallery uses uniform 72-by-72-point square cards in catalogue order (80 until a later change, which the owner asked for on 2026-11-23 as "a bit too big", a tenth smaller), with 6-point gaps and 12-point outer padding. The default panel is 174 points wide (190 with the 80-point cards), fitting two columns with equal side margins. A wider panel fits more of them: the widest the panel is made, 360 points, fits four columns where it fitted three. Sidebar resizing changes the number of columns, never individual card proportions or label-dependent sizes. Previews use a box of 60% of the card's side (43 points in the 72-point card, 48 points in the 80 it was) centred horizontally and vertically in each square. The same tile is drawn in a stencil, in its Quick Shapes group above the divider, in the Quick Shapes stencil and in the search results; the AutoConnect flyout has tiles of its own (44 points) that this does not change. Cards have no visible captions; hovering shows only the full shape name, without instructions. Names remain available to accessibility tools, inspection and the stencil filter. Filtering and switching stencils preserve the same card size. The accepted 1.35-point graphite contour, actual master geometry, imported image proportions and colours, and click/drag placement are preserved. Focus, hover, press and active-drag states remain distinct. The panel divider reserves its resize hit area from the adjacent ruler, so resizing does not create a guide; normal ruler dragging remains available.

A local filter searches only the current stencil's master names, case-insensitively. It processes at most 4,096 input Unicode characters, maps CR/LF to spaces, removes other controls and retains at most 128 characters. UI input and `window.shapeFilter {query}` share that policy. Switching stencils clears the filter; changed queries reset gallery scrolling. Filtering changes no document cells, revision or undo history. Typing, clipboard shortcuts and Escape stay with the field while it owns the keyboard; document/template changes synchronize the filter even while the panel is collapsed. Empty results have a clear action and explanation.

This is original DiaCraft UI presentation, not a change to document styling or a claim of complete catalogue search. **Shape search adds, on top of this:** the box reads **Search shapes** and has a magnifier at its right, typing still narrows the stencil on view by name, and **Enter or the magnifier searches every stencil**: the results are a stencil of their own (a row at the head of the stencil menu, with a cross that clears them) with each stencil's matches under its name. A stencil's first masters are its **Quick Shapes**, above a thin divider that has no label (an emptied group shows **Drop Quick Shapes here**), a **Quick Shapes** stencil gathers every open stencil's group under its name, a tile's right-click menu adds or removes one, a tile dragged and let go inside the window changes its group, **Reset Stencil** is a row of the stencil menu, and the AutoConnect flyout offers them. The Search for shapes and Quick Shapes parity rows are done. Native light/dark, compact/wide, picker/filter and placement checks accompany the implementation.
