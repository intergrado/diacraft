# Provenance

Where DiaCraft's non-original material comes from. A row's commit is the one the source repository's clone was at when its files were
read (from the clone's `.git/packed-refs`).
A row says what was copied, and means it. At every
tracked text file was compared with every text file of the clones, word by word (case and punctuation kept, runs of 7 or more words). **The rule the rows
follow:** a share of 15 or more words, and a file that shares most of its lines with a clone's file, has a row that names it, and
`xtask/tests/it/provenance.rs` lists those files, so that deleting a row fails a test (the MIT text in `NOTICE` apart: a licence is not a share). A shorter share of code (7 to 14 words, say a `TempDir` or a test logger) may be the
shape that an API or a standard helper gives, so a row names it only where its file has a row for another reason or a review saw it; the
shares of 10 to 14 words that no row names are listed in the report, outside the repository. Prose is held to more: a sentence that
a clone's page has too is named whatever its length. The comparison found such shares of 7 words or more in `AGENTS.md`
and `README.md`, and their rows name them (the `AGENTS.md` row every run of 9 words or more with any clone's `AGENTS.md`, and
those of 8 words with vectorcraft's). The pages in `docs/` share table headers and cells and the revision-history header, apart from the
sentence of `docs/gaps.md` that its row names (and the specification pages two phrases of ordinary English of 7 words, named in the
report).

## Reference app

The reference app has never been taken apart: nothing was read from an install, and behaviour comes from public information
only. The files DiaCraft writes are being tested by opening them in it, in LibreOffice Draw and in draw.io.
