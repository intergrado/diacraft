Privacy

You can draw, edit and save on your own computer without an account or an internet connection. DiaCraft sends no usage telemetry.

When the desktop app connects

  • Automation. Enabling the desktop control port lets programs on your machine operate the open drawing. The MCP command can use that port or an address you supply. In headless mode it talks to its client through standard input and output. If you use an AI client, its own settings govern what it sends to its provider.
  • Printing. When you send a job to a printer, DiaCraft hands it to your system’s print service. Your printer setup determines whether the job goes over a network.
  • Licence links. Clicking a licence name on the splash or About screen opens the licence publisher’s page in your browser. The Third-party licences link there opens no page: it shows a list that is built into the program.

When you use DiaCraft in your browser

  • Loading. The browser version is a site of its own, beside this one. Its page and the editor’s files come from that address, and nothing is loaded from another one. The editor comes as a few large files, which your browser may keep for later visits.
  • After it has loaded. The editor makes no network request but for two files of its own, which come from that same address the first time they are shown: the picture on the File view’s Home page and the list of licences. It sends no drawing, no usage data and no error report, and the page’s content security policy allows connections to that address only. It stores nothing in cookies, local storage or other browser storage, so closing or reloading the tab discards the drawing.
  • Files. A file you open or drop on the page is read by the editor on your computer, in your browser’s memory, and goes nowhere else. A file you save, export or print to PDF is made on your computer and handed to your browser as a download, or written to the file you chose. Nothing is uploaded, and the editor keeps no copy after you close the tab.
  • Licence links. As in the desktop app, clicking a licence name on the splash or About screen opens the licence publisher’s page in a new tab. The Third-party licences link opens no page.

What happens when you visit this site

Cloudflare Pages serves the pages, images and styles, and, from its own address, the browser version. Neither site sets cookies, stores tracking identifiers in your browser or runs analytics.

The only third-party request a page makes on load is an optional check of GitHub’s API for the latest release version and download links, which only the home page makes. The request contains no drawings or filenames from your computer. With scripts disabled, the release link still works. Following a download or repository link takes you to GitHub.
